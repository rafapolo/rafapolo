Dear Herzog & de Meuron Team,

I live in Basel, 5 minutes by bike from your office, hold a Swiss B permit and am available immediately, after a sabbatical year dedicated to my son, as a first-time father, and to personal projects. I am a Brazilian-Portuguese computer scientist with an MSc in Information Systems, focused on knowledge representation, and over twenty years I have worked for NGOs, newsrooms, startups and Swisscom. In Rio de Janeiro I co-founded and shared a co-working space with architects and designers for years, building projects and exhibitions together, including architecture exhibitions for MASP. In Berlin I spent two years at Tactical Tech building tools for Exposing the Invisible, which taught me to look at technology critically. What I enjoy most is that space between design and technology: a data challenge, an experimental solution, and all the work with other creative people.

More recently I supported a PhD in Urbanism with the analytics on 150 years of Brazilian census housing data. Architects have always been close to my work. You shape physical space; I have spent my career shaping and mapping cyberspace, a different kind of architecture: logical systems designed with formal languages.

My open analytics platform rodado.xyz is the closest thing I have to a preview of this role. A fleet of semi-autonomous AI agents runs the ETL pipelines that integrate nearly 1,800 Brazilian public tables, more than 1 TB of data that grows every week, into one of the largest mirrors of Brazilian public data. Most of the work was data modelling, inference and governance: none of the sources share keys, so I mapped the bridges between equivalent columns, gave every recurring metric one shared definition and built an ontology of the entities behind them. Today 85% of the datasets connect, as the atlas shows. An MCP server I wrote lets anyone's agents get correct SQL answers across all of it, and more than twenty published analytics platforms sit on top so people can navigate all this data. Its query layer, db.rodado.xyz, is in effect a lightweight lakehouse, and it was used as a data source by analysts during the last Brazilian elections.

Analytics only matters when people understand it, so I like to tell data as stories. cantography.ch, my spatial analytics of all 795,000 Swiss companies placed on their buildings since 1883, is competing in the Swiss Visualisation Award and opens with a 70-second guided tour with music generated, pushing the limits of what a web browser can do. Where Cantography shows where Swiss companies sit in physical space, HelvetiScan maps where they live in cyberspace: it scans all 2.5 million .ch domains in under three hours from a single machine, charting unpatched software, expired certificates, foreign dependencies and a sovereignty score for each domain. My open map of the Amazon geoglyphs, pre-Columbian earthworks that are architecture at the scale of the landscape, combines LiDAR findings from Nature and Science with more than 2,200 sites and tells their story in a two-minute tour. It is one of the experiments on my blog, ミxploring New Cartographic Technologies, where I push what AI can do with large amounts of data and how to represent them.

I see agents, MCP and careful harness engineering, the tests, guardrails and context around a model, offering real practical value for your practice: extracting structure from specifications and archives, and letting colleagues query and transform their own data in plain language, even just by talking.

A data platform is only as good as the people who use it and the questions they need answered. At EDGE Empower in Zug, as the first in-house technical lead, I replaced a legacy Python payment tool with a parallelised in-browser JavaScript one I wrote, cutting processing time by 98%. I also hired and coached three developers and a designer, and built a system so colleagues across teams could feed ideas into the roadmap. The best technical work is almost invisible: problems don't escalate, complexity doesn't accumulate, and the team is free to focus on the next challenge.

My years of design and technology are collected at extrapolo.com, and I warmly invite you to visit. I am a native Portuguese speaker, fluent in English and Spanish, with B1 German and A2 Greek from my years in Athens. My expected annual gross salary is CHF 120,000.

I look forward to meeting you, showing you what I have been building, and learning how I can best help your team.
